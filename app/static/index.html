How to read a result

Five things worth knowing before you trust any single prediction.

  1. The verdict is the class whose probability beat the decision threshold — a value fitted on validation data, not 0.5 by default.
  2. The gauge shows how far the score sits from that threshold. Within a whisker of it, the panel labels the call a close call.
  3. Evidence signals are physical measurements (grid energy, noise floor, edge balance, spectrum) compared with the distribution of authentic captures in the training data.
  4. Saliency maps exist only when the deployed model is a convolutional network, and then come from that model's own gradients.
  5. Generator naming exists only if the training corpus had per-generator labels. It answers UNKNOWN rather than guessing.

What this tool is

  • A supervised binary classifier (real vs AI-generated) trained on the repository dataset, with a documented 70/15/15 split.
  • An audit trail: model card, comparison table, re-measurement command, and reports that were produced by the same code that trained the model.
  • CPU-only and self-contained — one small artifact in models/ is all the service needs.

What it is not

  • Not proof of origin, and not a deepfake detector for video or for faces at other resolutions.
  • Not a semantic analysis: it cannot tell you who is in an image or what the prompt was.
  • Not robust by promise: compression, resizing, screenshots and unseen generators change the measurements.
